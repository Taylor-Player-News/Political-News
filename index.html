Immigration · Rulemaking

Student and exchange visas stop being open-ended tomorrow

A Department of Homeland Security final rule takes effect September 15, changing admission in the F (academic student), J (exchange visitor) and I (foreign media) classifications from “duration of status” to a fixed time period, and adding an extension-of-stay procedure. The rule amends 8 CFR parts 214, 248 and 274a.

DHS classified it as a major rule subject to congressional review, and wrote into the rule that if the effective date changes at the conclusion of that review, it will publish a further notice setting the actual date or terminating the rule. The stated purpose is to let DHS evaluate whether these nonimmigrants are maintaining status while in the United States.

  • DocketICEB–2025–0001, RIN 1653–AA95
  • StatusMajor rule, subject to congressional review

Appropriations · Public law

Agencies are funded to December 11 under a bill that started life as an Africa trade measure

H.R. 6500 became Public Law 119–103 on September 2, titled the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027. It provides fiscal year 2027 appropriations through December 11, 2026 and extends authorities for a range of programs including surface transportation and veterans programs.

The vehicle is worth noting: the Senate and House clerks still carry H.R. 6500 under its original subject, extending duty-free treatment for certain African imports under the African Growth and Opportunity Act and extending customs user fees. The continuing resolution was attached to that bill rather than moving on its own.

Appropriations · What the reporting adds

What the funding law reportedly carries beyond the topline

The law text is the authority on its contents, but the provisions drawing attention in coverage are a temporary block on an Office of Management and Budget proposal to route federal grant decisions through political appointees, a bar on shifting funds from other programs to Border Patrol, and deviations from current spending for WIC, disaster relief and wildland fire, with the Senate adding others including 2028 Olympic preparations and housing assistance.

Coverage also reports a two-year extension of economic pacts with sub-Saharan African countries and with Haiti, and that the grantmaking block runs only until December 11 — the same date the money runs out. Treat each of these as reported until you read them in the enrolled text.

Sources Reported Roll Call, Sept 1 Defense One, Sept 1 Bill text and actions, Congress.gov

Courts · Redistricting

The Supreme Court stayed a federal order in the Missouri map referendum fight, six days after declining to stay the state ruling

On September 10 the Court granted an application for stay in People Not Politicians v. Onder, No. 26A326, staying a September 8 order of the Eastern District of Missouri pending appeal in the Eighth Circuit and disposition of a certiorari petition. If certiorari is denied the stay ends automatically.

Read the sequence carefully, because the two rulings point in different directions. Per the stay application, the Missouri Supreme Court held on September 3 in von Glahn that the state constitution allows a referendum on the legislature’s congressional map, said HB 1 “is not the law and has never been the law,” and enjoined use of that map in November. The Secretary of State sought a stay of that ruling and the Supreme Court denied it on September 8. What the Court granted on September 10 was a stay of a separate federal district court order.

Courts · Campaign finance

Party committees keep the discount broadcast rate for now, on a jurisdictional ruling rather than the merits

In National Republican Congressional Committee v. Brown, No. 26A274, the Court on September 4 recalled and stayed the Fourth Circuit’s mandate in No. 26–1785. The Fourth Circuit had set aside an FCC public notice and, in the process, held that it had jurisdiction while the candidates’ application for review was still pending at the Commission.

The Court’s per curiam reasoning is statutory: under 47 U.S.C. §155(c)(7), filing an application for review is “a condition precedent to judicial review,” so the challengers had to wait for the Commission to resolve it by order. The underlying question — whether party and joint fundraising committees may pay the lowest unit charge that candidates get for political ads — is unresolved. Justice Jackson dissented.

Courts · Immigration

An October detention case vanished from the argument calendar after a state pardon

Genalo v. Black, No. 25–886, was dismissed September 11 under Rule 46 on a joint stipulation by all parties. The case asked at what point noncitizens in prolonged detention are entitled to a bond hearing, and what the government must show to keep holding them. It had been set for argument in October.

According to the Solicitor General’s letter to the Clerk, the Governor of New York granted the remaining respondent a full and unconditional pardon three calendar days before his merits brief was due, which removed the conviction underlying mandatory detention and mooted the habeas case. The letter states the government intends to look for alternative vehicles to get the same questions decided during October Term 2026.

Courts · Elections

Standing, not legality: the election order stay from late August

Worth keeping alongside the newer election cases. In Trump v. California, No. 26A124, decided August 24, the Court stayed a District of Massachusetts injunction against implementing an executive order on election integrity. The per curiam opinion rests on jurisdiction: because the order itself does not harm the states, they lacked standing, so the district court could not bar the government from trying to implement it.

The opinion is explicit that this is not a ruling that implementation will be lawful — in its own words, “time will tell.” A companion application, No. 26A139, was denied as moot.

Source Primary document Per curiam opinion, supremecourt.gov

Oversight · Public law

Four bills signed September 11, including a spending-disclosure expansion

The White House recorded four bills signed on September 11. The one with the widest reach is H.R. 2069, the Stop Secret Spending Act of 2025, which extends federal spending transparency requirements to other transaction agreements — a contracting instrument that sits outside normal procurement rules — and modifies the related standards, oversight and reporting.

The others: H.R. 5366, the Doug LaMalfa Federal Disaster Tax Relief Certainty Act, extending and codifying the deduction for qualified disaster-related personal casualty losses and the income exclusion for qualified wildfire relief payments; H.R. 2196, extending the National Emergency Medical Services Memorial Foundation’s authority to build a commemorative work in the District of Columbia; and H.R. 1276, removing deed restrictions from a 3.62-acre parcel conveyed to Paducah, Kentucky.

Energy · Rulemaking

Federal buildings get another six months before clean-energy standards bite

On September 2 the Department of Energy further stayed the compliance date for the fossil-fuel reduction requirements covering new and majorly renovated federal buildings, in 10 CFR part 433 subpart B and part 435 subpart B, pushing it to March 1, 2027. Those provisions were published in May 2024 and have now been stayed three times, previously to September 1, 2026.

DOE says its review of the rule and its implementation guidance is still ongoing, and states plainly that agencies are not required to comply with the standards while the compliance date is stayed.

Source Primary document Federal Register, Sept 2

South Carolina’s 1st District

An open seat on the coast, and the only South Carolina district that behaves like a swing seat

SC–01 has no incumbent on the ballot November 3. The general election field is Nancy Lacore (Democrat), Jenny Costa Honeycutt (Republican), Bill Reeside (Libertarian) and Margo Ellis. The district takes in all of Beaufort and Berkeley counties and parts of Charleston, Colleton, Dorchester and Jasper.

Both nominations went to runoffs on June 23. Reporting has Honeycutt, a Charleston County Council member, defeating state Representative Mark Smith 54–46, and Lacore, a former Navy admiral, defeating attorney Mac Deford 52–48, on unofficial returns. Seventeen candidates ran across the two primaries. The Cook Political Report rates the district R+6, and it has gone Democratic once in four decades, in 2018.

Elections · Mail ballots

Can the Postal Service rule on mail-in ballots take full effect before November?

The administration asked the Supreme Court on September 6 to let it fully implement a new Postal Service rule on mail-in ballots. Reporting describes the dispute as fully briefed, with a decision possible at any time.

What would resolve it: an order in United States Postal Service v. California on the supremecourt.gov docket. Until that posts, nothing about the rule’s status in November is settled.

Campaign finance

Who may pay the lowest unit charge for political advertising?

The September 4 stay decided that the Fourth Circuit acted too early, not who is entitled to the rate. The FCC has yet to resolve the candidates’ application for review by order, which is the step the Court identified as a condition precedent to judicial review.

What would resolve it: an FCC order on the application for review, then a properly timed petition for judicial review — or a grant of certiorari in No. 26A274.

Missouri’s November congressional map
